# Administrative Controls

## What they are

Administrative controls are security controls implemented through **policies, procedures, processes, and management decisions**.

They help establish what people are required or expected to do to protect systems and information.

## Examples

- Security policies
- Access approval procedures
- Security awareness training
- Separation of duties
- Requiring multiple people to approve an important operation
- Employee background checks
- Incident response procedures
- Password policies

## Example

A company creates a policy stating:

> New employees must have their manager approve access before an account is created.

This is an administrative control because the protection is based on an organizational policy and process.

The company could then use a technical system to enforce the policy.

## Administrative vs. Technical vs. Physical

### Administrative

Uses **rules, policies, and procedures**.

**Example:** Manager must approve new-user access.

### Technical

Uses **technology**.

**Example:** A system requires the manager's approval before creating the account.

### Physical

Uses **physical protection**.

**Example:** A locked server room.

Organizations often use all three types together.

## Separation of Duties

Separation of duties means dividing important responsibilities between multiple people.

### Example

One employee requests a large payment.

Another employee must approve it.

This reduces the chance that one person can complete the entire sensitive process without oversight.

### Remember

**Administrative = Rules and processes**

## What I Learned

Administrative controls use organizational policies, procedures, and processes to manage security. They are often combined with technical and physical controls to provide multiple layers of protection.
