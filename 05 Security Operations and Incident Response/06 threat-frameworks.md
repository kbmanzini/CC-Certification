# Threat Frameworks

## 1. What Is a Threat Framework?

A threat framework provides an organized way to describe, understand, assess, or communicate cybersecurity threats.

Frameworks help security teams use consistent terminology and methods.

## 2. MITRE ATT&CK

MITRE ATT&CK is a knowledge base of adversary behaviors based on observed real-world activity.

It organizes information into:
- Tactics
- Techniques
- Sub-techniques
- Procedures

**Tactic:** Describes an adversary's goal or reason for an action.

**Technique:** Describes how an adversary attempts to achieve that goal.

**Procedure:** Describes a specific implementation of a technique.

Example:
An adversary may have the tactic of gaining initial access and use a technique involving a compromised account.

MITRE ATT&CK helps organizations:
- Understand adversary behavior
- Evaluate security coverage
- Improve detection strategies
- Organize threat intelligence
- Identify defensive gaps

## 3. Cyber Kill Chain

The Cyber Kill Chain is a model that describes stages commonly associated with a cyber intrusion.

A commonly taught version includes:

1. Reconnaissance
2. Weaponization
3. Delivery
4. Exploitation
5. Installation
6. Command and Control
7. Actions on Objectives

Defenders can use the model to consider where an intrusion may be detected or interrupted.

## 4. MITRE ATT&CK vs. Cyber Kill Chain

| MITRE ATT&CK | Cyber Kill Chain |
|---|---|
| Detailed knowledge base of adversary behaviors | High-level sequence of intrusion stages |
| Organizes tactics and techniques | Organizes stages in an intrusion |
| Helps map observed behavior | Helps understand an intrusion's progression |

They are related but are not interchangeable.

## 5. NIST Cybersecurity Framework

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) helps organizations manage cybersecurity risk.

Its six functions in CSF 2.0 are:

- **Govern:** Establish and monitor cybersecurity risk strategy and oversight.
- **Identify:** Understand assets, risks, and the environment.
- **Protect:** Implement safeguards.
- **Detect:** Discover possible cybersecurity events.
- **Respond:** Take action regarding detected incidents.
- **Recover:** Restore capabilities and services.

Remember: CSF 2.0 added the Govern function.

## 6. Frameworks Are Not the Same as Policies

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Framework | Organizes security practices and risk management |
| Policy | States organizational requirements and direction |
| Standard | Defines mandatory requirements |
| Procedure | Explains how to perform a task |
| Guideline | Provides recommended practices |

## 7. Choosing and Using Frameworks

Organizations select frameworks based on:
- Business needs
- Industry
- Regulatory requirements
- Risk level
- Available resources
- Existing security processes

Frameworks should be adapted to the organization's environment.

## Key Takeaways

- MITRE ATT&CK organizes adversary tactics and techniques.
- The Cyber Kill Chain describes stages of an intrusion.
- NIST CSF 2.0 has six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover.
- Frameworks provide structure but do not replace organizational policies and procedures.
