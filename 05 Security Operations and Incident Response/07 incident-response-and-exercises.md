# Incident Response and Exercises

## 1. What Is Incident Response?

Incident response is the organized process an organization follows to prepare for, identify, manage, and recover from cybersecurity incidents.

Goals include:
- Limiting damage
- Protecting information
- Restoring normal operations
- Preserving useful evidence
- Learning from incidents

## 2. What Is an Incident Response Plan?

An Incident Response Plan (IRP) documents how an organization will respond to security incidents.

It should identify:
- Roles and responsibilities
- Reporting procedures
- Communication methods
- Escalation requirements
- Decision-making authority
- Documentation requirements
- Recovery and follow-up activities

## 3. Common Incident Response Phases

A commonly taught incident response lifecycle includes:

### Preparation
- Establish policies and procedures.
- Assign responsibilities.
- Train personnel.
- Prepare communication and response resources.

### Detection and Analysis
- Identify suspicious activity.
- Review alerts and logs.
- Determine whether an incident occurred.
- Assess severity and scope.

### Containment
- Limit the incident's impact.
- Protect unaffected systems.
- Follow approved response procedures.

### Eradication
- Address the cause of the incident.
- Remove malicious or unauthorized changes.
- Correct relevant weaknesses.

### Recovery
- Restore affected systems and services.
- Confirm systems are functioning as expected.
- Monitor for signs of recurring problems.

### Lessons Learned
- Review what happened.
- Identify what worked and what did not.
- Update procedures and training.
- Record improvement actions.

## 4. Incident Response Roles

Possible roles include:
- Incident response manager
- Security analysts
- IT administrators
- Legal representatives
- Human resources
- Communications staff
- Executive leadership

The exact team depends on the organization.

## 5. Communication During an Incident

Communication should be:
- Accurate
- Timely
- Limited to appropriate recipients
- Consistent with organizational policy
- Documented when required

Sensitive incident details should not be shared with unauthorized people.

## 6. Evidence Handling

Evidence may include:
- System logs
- Security alerts
- Relevant files
- Network records
- Access records
- Incident timelines

Evidence should be handled according to organizational procedures.

Important concepts:
- **Integrity:** Evidence should not be improperly changed.
- **Chain of custody:** A record of who handled evidence and when.
- **Documentation:** Records how evidence was collected, stored, and transferred.

## 7. Incident Response Exercises

Exercises help organizations determine whether their plans and teams are prepared.

### Tabletop Exercise
Participants discuss how they would respond to a fictional scenario.

### Walkthrough
Participants review a process step by step to identify gaps.

### Simulation
An organization acts through a planned scenario to evaluate its response.

### Technical Exercise
Authorized teams test technical detection and response capabilities in a controlled environment.

## 8. Why Exercises Matter

Exercises can reveal:
- Unclear responsibilities
- Missing contact information
- Communication problems
- Gaps in procedures
- Training needs
- Delays in decision-making

## 9. Incident Response vs. Disaster Recovery

| Incident Response | Disaster Recovery |
|---|---|
| Manages security incidents | Restores IT systems and services after disruption |
| Focuses on identifying and handling incidents | Focuses on restoring technology |
| May involve containment and eradication | May involve restoring backups and alternate systems |

## Key Takeaways

- Preparation happens before an incident.
- Detection and analysis determine what happened.
- Containment limits impact.
- Eradication addresses the cause.
- Recovery restores services.
- Lessons learned improve future response.
- Tabletop exercises help evaluate plans without requiring a live incident.
