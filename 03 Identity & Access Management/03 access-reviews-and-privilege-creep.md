# Access Reviews and Privilege Creep

## Access Reviews

An access review checks whether users still have the permissions they need.

Organizations should perform reviews periodically and after major changes.

Examples of changes include:

- Job transfers
- Promotions
- Department changes
- Terminations
- Changes in responsibilities

---

## Questions in an Access Review

Security teams may ask:

- Does this user still work here?
- Does the user still need this access?
- Has the user's job changed?
- Are the permissions excessive?
- Are inactive accounts still enabled?
- Are service accounts still required?

---

## Privilege Creep

Privilege creep occurs when users accumulate permissions over time that they no longer need.

### Example

An employee starts in accounting.

Later, they transfer to IT.

The organization gives them IT permissions but forgets to remove their old accounting permissions.

The employee now has access to both systems.

This is privilege creep.

---

## Risks of Privilege Creep

Privilege creep can result in:

- Excessive access
- Increased attack surface
- Greater impact if an account is compromised
- Accidental misuse
- Difficulty determining appropriate permissions

---

## Preventing Privilege Creep

Organizations can:

- Conduct regular access reviews
- Use role-based access control
- Follow least privilege
- Remove old permissions
- Properly deprovision accounts
- Review access after role changes
