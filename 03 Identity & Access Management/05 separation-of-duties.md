# Separation of Duties

## Definition

Separation of Duties (SoD) means dividing important responsibilities among multiple people so that one person does not have complete control over a sensitive process.

---

## Example

An employee requests a financial payment.

A different employee approves the payment.

This creates separation of duties.

---

## Why Separation of Duties Matters

It can reduce the risk of:

- Fraud
- Abuse of privileges
- Unauthorized changes
- Conflicts of interest
- One person having complete control

---

## Least Privilege vs. Separation of Duties

| Least Privilege | Separation of Duties |
|---|---|
| Limits permissions | Divides responsibilities |
| Focuses on access | Focuses on tasks and roles |
| "Only what you need" | "Don't give one person all the control" |

---

## Example

### Without Separation of Duties

One employee:

1. Creates a payment
2. Approves the payment
3. Sends the payment

This creates excessive control.

### With Separation of Duties

Employee A creates the payment.

Employee B approves it.

This provides additional oversight.

---

## Memory Trick

**Least Privilege = Limit access**

**Separation of Duties = Split responsibilities**
