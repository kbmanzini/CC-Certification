# Chapter 2 Review: Incident Response, Business Continuity, and Disaster Recovery

## Key Terms

| Term                           | Meaning                                                                    |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Backup                         | A separate copy of data used for recovery                                  |
| Business Continuity (BC)       | Maintaining essential business functions during a disruption               |
| Business Continuity Plan (BCP) | Procedures for maintaining essential business operations                   |
| Business Impact Analysis (BIA) | Evaluating how disruptions affect critical business functions              |
| Disaster Recovery (DR)         | Restoring IT systems, infrastructure, applications, and data               |
| Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)   | Documented procedures for technology recovery                              |
| Security Event                 | An observable occurrence involving a system, network, application, or user |
| Security Incident              | An event that threatens or affects security and requires a response        |
| Incident Response              | The organized process for managing security incidents                      |
| RTO                            | Target time for restoring a service                                        |
| RPO                            | Target for acceptable data loss measured in time                           |
| Tabletop Exercise              | A discussion-based practice exercise                                       |

## Quick Comparisons

### Business Continuity vs. Disaster Recovery

* BC: Keep essential business operations going.
* DR: Restore technology and data.

### BIA vs. Risk Assessment

* BIA: Understand the consequences of interrupted business functions.
* Risk assessment: Identify and evaluate risks.

### Event vs. Incident

* Event: Something observable happened.
* Incident: An occurrence that threatens or affects security and requires a response.

### RTO vs. RPO

* RTO: How quickly a service should be restored.
* RPO: How much recent data loss can be tolerated.

### Full vs. Incremental vs. Differential Backup

* Full: Copies all selected data.
* Incremental: Copies changes since the latest backup.
* Differential: Copies changes since the latest full backup.

## Practice Questions

### 1. An organization wants to continue serving customers while its primary office is unavailable. What concept applies?

**Answer:** Business Continuity.

### 2. An organization needs to restore its databases and servers after a major outage. What concept applies?

**Answer:** Disaster Recovery.

### 3. Which process identifies critical business functions and evaluates the consequences of disruption?

**Answer:** Business Impact Analysis.

### 4. A company wants to limit how long a service remains unavailable. Which objective is relevant?

**Answer:** Recovery Time Objective (RTO).

### 5. A company wants to limit the amount of recent data it might lose. Which objective is relevant?

**Answer:** Recovery Point Objective (RPO).

### 6. A team discusses how it would respond to a simulated incident without performing a full technical recovery. What is this called?

**Answer:** Tabletop Exercise.

### 7. A monitoring system records a failed login attempt. Is this automatically a security incident?

**Answer:** No. It is an event that may need to be evaluated in context.

### 8. Which backup type copies changes made since the most recent backup?

**Answer:** Incremental Backup.

### 9. Which incident response activity aims to limit the spread or impact of an incident?

**Answer:** Containment.

### 10. Why should an organization test its recovery plans?

**Answer:** To find weaknesses, verify procedures, and improve readiness before a real disruption.

## Final Summary

* Business continuity focuses on maintaining essential business functions.
* Disaster recovery focuses on restoring IT systems and data.
* A BIA helps prioritize business functions and recovery needs.
* Incident response coordinates the handling of security incidents.
* RTO concerns restoration time.
* RPO concerns tolerable data loss.
* Backups provide copies of data for recovery.
* Exercises and tests reveal weaknesses in plans.

## What I Learned

Incident response, business continuity, and disaster recovery help an organization prepare for disruptions, respond to incidents, protect critical information, restore services, and improve its plans over time.
