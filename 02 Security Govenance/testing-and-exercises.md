# Testing and Exercises

## Why Test Plans?

An organization may have an incident response plan, business continuity plan, or disaster recovery plan, but the plan may not work as expected until it is tested.

Testing helps identify missing information, unclear responsibilities, technical problems, and unrealistic recovery expectations.

## Tabletop Exercise

A tabletop exercise is a discussion-based activity in which participants talk through a simulated situation.

Participants explain what they would do and which procedures they would follow.

### Example

A team discusses how it would respond if a critical business application became unavailable.

**Key idea:** Discussion, not a full technical recovery.

## Walkthrough

A walkthrough involves reviewing a plan or procedure step by step to check that participants understand their responsibilities and the sequence of actions.

## Simulation

A simulation models a scenario so participants can practice decisions and responses.

The degree of technical activity depends on the exercise design.

## Technical or Recovery Test

A technical test may verify that systems, backups, failover mechanisms, or restoration procedures actually work.

Tests should be planned carefully to avoid causing unnecessary disruption.

## Lessons Learned

After an exercise or real incident, participants should document:

* What worked
* What did not work
* Which steps were unclear
* What resources were missing
* What should be improved
* Who is responsible for follow-up actions

## CC Exam Tips

* Tabletop exercise = discuss a scenario.
* Walkthrough = review procedures step by step.
* Technical test = verify that a technical capability works.
* Lessons learned = identify and document improvements.

## What I Learned

Testing helps organizations find weaknesses in their plans before a real disruption occurs. Plans should be updated when exercises reveal problems or circumstances change.
