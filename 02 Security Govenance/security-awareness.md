# Security Awareness

## What Is Security Awareness?

Security awareness means understanding common cybersecurity threats and knowing how to behave safely when using computers, networks, applications, and information.

Employees and users are an important part of an organization's security because human mistakes can create opportunities for attackers.

## Why Security Awareness Matters

Security awareness helps people recognize threats, protect sensitive information, and follow security policies.

It can help reduce risks involving:

* Phishing emails
* Social engineering
* Weak or reused passwords
* Unauthorized access
* Malware
* Accidental data exposure
* Unsafe internet browsing

## Common Security Threats

### Phishing

Phishing is an attempt to trick someone into revealing information, opening a malicious attachment, or visiting a harmful website.

**Example:** An email pretends to come from a bank and asks the recipient to verify their password through a suspicious link.

**Protection:** Verify the sender, inspect unexpected requests, and report suspicious messages through approved channels.

### Social Engineering

Social engineering uses deception or manipulation to persuade someone to reveal information or perform an action.

**Example:** Someone calls an employee pretending to be IT support and requests their login credentials.

**Protection:** Verify identities and follow established procedures before sharing information or granting access.

### Tailgating

Tailgating occurs when an unauthorized person follows an authorized person into a restricted physical area without proper authorization.

**Protection:** Follow visitor procedures and physical access policies rather than allowing unknown people into restricted areas.

### Password Risks

Weak, reused, or exposed passwords can allow unauthorized access to accounts.

**Protection:**

* Use unique passwords.
* Use a password manager approved for your organization.
* Enable multifactor authentication (MFA) when available.
* Never share passwords.
* Report suspected account compromise.

## Security Awareness Training

Organizations use security awareness training to teach employees and other users how to recognize and respond to threats.

Training may cover:

* Phishing recognition
* Password safety
* Handling sensitive information
* Reporting security incidents
* Acceptable use of technology
* Physical security
* Privacy requirements
* Safe remote work practices

Training should be updated as threats and organizational requirements change.

## Reporting Suspicious Activity

Users should know how to report suspicious emails, unexpected login prompts, lost devices, possible data exposure, and other security concerns.

Reporting promptly allows the appropriate personnel to investigate and respond.

Do not assume that reporting a suspicious event is unnecessary just because you are unsure whether it is a real threat.

## Security Awareness vs. Technical Controls

### Security Awareness

Helps people recognize threats and make safer decisions.

**Example:** Teaching employees how to recognize phishing.

### Technical Controls

Use technology to prevent, detect, or respond to threats.

**Example:** An email filter blocks known malicious messages.

Organizations often combine awareness training with technical controls.

## CC Exam Tips

* Phishing = deceptive messages designed to trick recipients.
* Social engineering = manipulating people to obtain information or actions.
* Tailgating = unauthorized physical entry by following someone.
* Security awareness training = teaching users to recognize threats and follow security practices.
* Reporting suspicious activity helps organizations respond to potential incidents.

## What I Learned

Security awareness helps people recognize cybersecurity threats and make informed decisions. Training, safe behavior, and timely reporting work alongside technical controls to protect an organization.
