# Security Governance Review

## Key Terms

| Term                           | Meaning                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Governance                     | How an organization directs and oversees security                   |
| Risk Management                | Identifying, evaluating, treating, and monitoring risks             |
| Compliance                     | Following applicable laws, regulations, contracts, and requirements |
| Policy                         | A high-level statement of organizational rules and expectations     |
| Standard                       | A specific, often mandatory requirement                             |
| Procedure                      | Step-by-step instructions for performing an activity                |
| Guideline                      | Recommended advice or practices                                     |
| Framework                      | Organized guidance for managing security or risk                    |
| Security Awareness             | Understanding threats and practicing safe behavior                  |
| Security Training              | Teaching people how to follow security practices                    |
| Business Impact Analysis (BIA) | Evaluating the effects of disruption on business functions          |
| Risk Assessment                | Identifying and evaluating potential risks                          |

## Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)

### Governance

Establishes security responsibilities, oversight, and decision-making processes.

### Risk Management

Helps an organization understand and handle risks.

Common approaches include:

* **Avoid:** Stop the activity that creates the risk.
* **Mitigate:** Reduce the likelihood or impact.
* **Transfer:** Shift some financial risk to another party.
* **Accept:** Acknowledge and retain the risk.

### Compliance

Ensures applicable requirements are identified and followed.

Examples include laws, regulations, contractual requirements, and organizational policies.

## Policies vs. Standards vs. Procedures vs. Guidelines

* **Policy:** States what is expected.
* **Standard:** Specifies a requirement.
* **Procedure:** Explains how to perform a task.
* **Guideline:** Suggests a recommended approach.

### Example

* Policy: Employees must protect company information.
* Standard: Work accounts must use approved authentication requirements.
* Procedure: Steps for requesting access to a company system.
* Guideline: Recommendations for creating memorable, unique passphrases.

## Security Controls

### Administrative Controls

Use policies, procedures, training, and organizational processes.

**Example:** Security awareness training.

### Technical Controls

Use technology to protect or monitor systems.

**Example:** A firewall or access control system.

### Physical Controls

Protect buildings, equipment, and physical locations.

**Example:** A locked server room.

## Security Awareness

Security awareness helps users recognize threats such as phishing and social engineering, protect credentials, and report suspicious activity.

Remember that people, processes, and technology all contribute to security.

## Quick Review Questions

### 1. What does GRC stand for?

**Answer:** Governance, Risk Management, and Compliance.

### 2. Which GRC component involves following applicable legal and contractual requirements?

**Answer:** Compliance.

### 3. What is the difference between a policy and a procedure?

**Answer:** A policy states expectations; a procedure explains how to perform an activity.

### 4. Which risk treatment reduces the likelihood or impact of a risk?

**Answer:** Mitigation.

### 5. Which control type includes security awareness training?

**Answer:** Administrative control.

### 6. Which control type includes a firewall?

**Answer:** Technical control.

### 7. Which control type includes a locked server room?

**Answer:** Physical control.

### 8. What is phishing?

**Answer:** A deceptive attempt to trick someone into revealing information or performing an unsafe action.

### 9. What is social engineering?

**Answer:** Using deception or manipulation to influence people into revealing information or taking actions.

### 10. Why should employees report suspicious activity?

**Answer:** Prompt reporting helps the organization investigate and respond to potential security incidents.

### 11. What is a security framework?

**Answer:** Organized guidance that helps an organization manage security risks and activities.

### 12. What is the purpose of a Business Impact Analysis?

**Answer:** To understand how disruptions affect critical business functions and help prioritize recovery.

* Risk management = Identify and handle risk.
* Compliance = Follow requirements.
* Policy = What is expected.
* Standard = Required specification.
* Procedure = How to do it.
* Guideline = Recommended approach.
* Administrative = People, rules, and processes.
* Technical = Technology.
* Physical = Buildings and equipment.

## What I Learned

Security governance establishes how an organization manages and oversees security. Risk management helps address potential harm, compliance addresses applicable requirements, and awareness training helps people recognize threats and follow security practices.
