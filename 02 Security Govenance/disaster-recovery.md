# Disaster Recovery (DR)

## What Is Disaster Recovery?

Disaster recovery is the process of restoring IT systems, applications, infrastructure, and data after a major disruption.

The focus is on recovering technology and information so that normal operations can resume.

## Possible Causes

* Hardware failure
* Power outages
* Natural disasters
* Fire or water damage
* Software failures
* Cybersecurity incidents
* Accidental damage to data

## Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

A Disaster Recovery Plan documents how an organization will restore its technology and data after a disruption.

A DRP may include:

* Recovery responsibilities
* System restoration procedures
* Backup locations
* Hardware and software requirements
* Network recovery steps
* Communication procedures
* Recovery priorities
* Contact information for key personnel

## Recovery Sites

Organizations may use alternate locations to restore systems.

### Hot Site

A prepared alternate location with substantial infrastructure already available.

**General idea:** Faster recovery, typically with higher ongoing costs.

### Warm Site

An alternate location with some infrastructure available but requiring additional setup or restoration.

**General idea:** A balance between preparation, cost, and recovery time.

### Cold Site

A location with basic facilities but little or no ready-to-use IT infrastructure.

**General idea:** Usually lower ongoing costs, but recovery may take longer.

The exact capabilities of these sites depend on the organization's arrangements.

## Example

A company's primary data center becomes unavailable. The IT team restores systems from backups and brings services online at an alternate location.

This is disaster recovery.

## Business Continuity vs. Disaster Recovery

* Business continuity: Maintain essential business functions.
* Disaster recovery: Restore IT systems and data.
