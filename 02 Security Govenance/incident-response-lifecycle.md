# Incident Response Lifecycle

## Overview

An incident response lifecycle organizes the work needed to prepare for, identify, contain, resolve, and learn from security incidents.

The exact phases and names may vary between frameworks and organizations.

## 1. Preparation

Before an incident occurs, an organization establishes its plans, tools, responsibilities, and procedures.

Examples:

* Create an incident response plan
* Train response personnel
* Establish reporting channels
* Prepare monitoring and logging systems
* Practice response procedures

## 2. Detection and Analysis

The organization identifies suspicious activity and determines whether an incident may have occurred.

Examples:

* Review alerts
* Analyze logs
* Validate reports
* Determine which systems may be affected
* Estimate the incident's scope and severity

## 3. Containment

Containment aims to limit the spread or impact of an incident.

Actions depend on the situation and the organization's approved procedures.

The goal is to prevent additional harm while preserving the ability to investigate and recover.

## 4. Eradication

Eradication removes the cause of the incident or addresses the underlying compromise.

Depending on the incident, this may involve removing malicious components, correcting vulnerabilities, or addressing compromised accounts.

## 5. Recovery

Recovery restores affected systems and services to normal or approved operating conditions.

The organization should verify that systems are functioning correctly and monitor for signs of continued problems.

## 6. Lessons Learned

After the incident, the organization reviews what happened and identifies improvements.

Questions may include:

* What happened?
* How was the incident detected?
* What worked well?
* What caused delays?
* What should change?
* Are additional controls or training needed?

## Important Idea

Incident response is not only about fixing the immediate problem. It also includes preparation, coordination, recovery, and improvement.

## CC Exam Tips

Remember this general sequence:

**Prepare → Detect and Analyze → Contain → Eradicate → Recover → Learn**

Some frameworks combine or rename phases. Focus on the purpose of each activity.

## What I Learned

The incident response lifecycle helps an organization respond consistently, limit damage, restore services, and improve its response capabilities over time.
